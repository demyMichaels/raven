Physics by Acceleration

⚡
Acceleration
vector · m/s²
a→ = Δv→ Δt
Rate of change of velocity — a vector, just like velocity.
📊
Average acceleration
over an interval
a→ avg = v→2 − v→1 t2 − t1
Change in velocity divided by the time interval.
📈
Instantaneous acceleration
limit Δt → 0
a→ = lim Δt→0 Δv→ Δt = dv→ dt
Slope of the velocity–time curve at a single instant.
➡️
Constant acceleration
a⃗ = const
a→ = constant
The kinematic equations apply: v = v₀ + at, x = v₀t + ½at², v² = v₀² + 2ax.
🛑
Deceleration
a⃗ opposite v⃗
a→ · v→ < 0 ⟹ slowing down
"Deceleration" is not a separate quantity — it's a⃗ opposite to v⃗.
📉
Jerk
da/dt
j→ = da→ dt = d3x→ dt3
3rd derivative of position. What you feel as a "lurch."
🫨
Jounce (snap)
dj/dt
s→ = dj→ dt = d4x→ dt4
4th derivative of position — what you feel as a "jolt."
🍎
Gravitational acceleration g
≈ 9.81 m/s²
g= GM r2 ≈ 9.81 m/s2
On Earth's surface. Points toward the planet's center.
⬇️
Free fall
a = g
a→ = g→ , v=gt , h= 12 gt2
All masses fall at the same rate (in vacuum).
📐
Standard gravity g₀
defined constant
g0 = 9.80665 m/s2 (exactly)
A defined constant since 1901. Earth's actual g varies by latitude and altitude.
🌍
Gravitational field strength
N/kg = m/s²
g→ = F→ m = − GM r2 r^
Same number as acceleration in free fall — that's why units match.
🧲
Newton's second law
a = F / m
F→ = ma→ ⟺ a→ = F→ m
Same force on a larger mass produces smaller acceleration.
🎯
Centripetal acceleration
points to center
ac = v2 r = ω2 r
Always points toward the center — magnitude is constant in uniform circular motion.
↗️
Tangential acceleration
along path
at = d|v→| dt = αr
Changes the speed, not the direction.
➕
Total acceleration
a_c ⊥ a_t
a→ = a→c + a→t , |a→| = ac2 + at2
Two perpendicular components — Pythagorean magnitude.
🔄
Angular acceleration α
rad/s²
α = dω dt = d2θ dt2
Rate of change of angular velocity — same α for all points on a rigid body.
📏
Linear from angular
a = αr
at = αr
Tangential acceleration grows with radius — farther points move faster.
🌀
Centrifugal acceleration
fictitious
a→cf = ω2 r→ (rotating frame only)
Not a real force — only appears in a rotating reference frame.
🌐
Coriolis acceleration
fictitious
a→cor = − 2 ω→ × v→
Deflects moving objects on a rotating Earth — right in N hemisphere, left in S.
🎢
Euler acceleration
changing ω
a→eul = dω→ dt × r→
Appears when angular velocity changes — you feel it as a sideways tug.
📱
Proper acceleration
accelerometer reading
aproper = |a→−g→|
What you actually feel — a free-falling accelerometer reads zero.
🧭
Coordinate acceleration
frame-dependent
a→coord = d2x→ dt2 (depends on frame)
Proper acceleration is frame-invariant; coordinate acceleration is not.
🎢
g-force (load factor)
n = a/g
n = aproper g0
1 g = resting. 0 g = free fall. 3 g = tight loop. 5 g ≈ blackout threshold.
🌊
Simple harmonic acceleration
a = −ω²x
a=−ω2x , ω=km
Restoring acceleration always points back toward equilibrium.
🪀
Pendulum acceleration
a_t = −g sin θ
at = −gsinθ ≈θ≪1 −gLθ
Small-angle approximation gives SHM with ω = √(g/L).
⚡
Relativistic acceleration
γ³ factor
F→ = γ3 ma→∥ or γm a→⊥
As v→c, γ→∞ — acceleration needs unbounded force.
🛑
Braking deceleration
everyday
a = v0−v t e.g. ≈ −9 m/s2
60 mph → 0 in 3 s ≈ −9 m/s² ≈ −0.9 g. Typical road friction limit is ≈ 1 g.
🏎️
Maximum road acceleration
0–60 in 2.5 s
a = 26.8m/s 2.5s ≈ 10.7 m/s2 ≈ 1.1 g
Limited by tyre grip: a_max ≈ μ·g ≈ 1 g for road tyres.
🌀
Planck acceleration
≈ 5.6 × 10⁵¹ m/s²
aP = c tP = c7 ℏG ≈ 5.56×1051 m/s2
The acceleration scale where quantum gravity becomes relevant.
🪐
Surface gravity comparison
g = GM/R²
g = GM R2
Moon ≈ 1.6 · Mars ≈ 3.7 · Earth ≈ 9.8 · Jupiter ≈ 24.8 m/s².
