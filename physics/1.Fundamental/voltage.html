Physics by Potential Difference

Voltage is a scalar potential · Field is a vector pointing downhill

⚡
Potential difference
scalar · volt (V)
V= Wq
Work per unit charge. Voltage is a scalar — no direction.
⊕
Electric potential of a point charge
scalar field
V= kQr = 14πε0 Qr
Equipotential surfaces are spheres — always perpendicular to field lines.
🔋
Electromotive force (emf)
ε · volt
ε= Wnon-el q
Despite the name, emf is not a force — it is energy per unit charge.
🔌
Terminal voltage
V_t = ε − Ir
Vt = ε−Ir
The voltage you actually measure at the battery's terminals when current flows.
📉
Voltage drop
across a component
Vdrop = IR
Energy lost per unit charge as it passes through the resistor.
🔻
Internal resistance drop
ε − V_t
ε−Vt = Ir
More current → more internal loss → lower terminal voltage.
➕
Voltages in series
additive
Vtot = V1 + V2 + …
Series sources add — this is how batteries make higher voltages.
＝
Voltage in parallel
same across branches
V1 = V2 = V
Parallel branches share the same voltage — not the same current.
📐
Ohm's law
V = IR
V=IR , I= VR , R= VI
V between the two ends of the resistor; I through it.
🔁
Kirchhoff's voltage law
ΣV = 0
∑loop V = 0
Sum of voltage rises = sum of voltage drops around any closed loop.
⚖️
Voltage divider
V_out from V_in
Vout = Vin · R2 R1 + R2
Voltage divides in proportion to resistance in a series chain.
📐
Potential gradient
E = −dV/dx
E→ = − dV dx x^
Field points in the direction of decreasing potential.
⬜
Uniform field from voltage
E = V/d
E= Vd
Between parallel plates, E is uniform and equals V/d (volts per metre).
💥
Potential energy from voltage
U = qV
U=qV
A charge at higher potential has more potential energy (for +q).
🔋
Capacitor voltage
V = Q/C
V= QC
More charge or smaller capacitance → higher voltage.
⚡
Energy stored in a capacitor
U = ½CV²
U= 12 CV2 = Q22C = 12 QV
Energy grows with the square of voltage — doubling V quadruples U.
⚡
Breakdown voltage
dielectric limit
Vbd = Ebd d
Above this voltage, the insulator ionizes and sparks. Air ≈ 3 MV/m.
🧷
Zener voltage
reference V_Z
VZ = constant
In reverse breakdown, the Zener holds V_Z — used for voltage references.
➡️
Diode forward voltage
≈ 0.7 V (Si)
VF ≈ 0.7 V (Si) | 0.3 V (Ge)
A "0.7 V rule" for silicon; 0.3 V for germanium. Real value is logarithmic in I.
🔲
Threshold voltage (MOSFET)
V_th
VGS > Vth ⟹ channel conducts
Below V_th the MOSFET is off; above it conducts. Modern CMOS ≈ 0.3–0.5 V.
🧲
Hall voltage
V_H = IB/(nqt)
VH = IBnqt
Used to measure carrier density and magnetic field.
🌡️
Thermal voltage
V_T = kT/q
VT = kTq ≈ 25.85 mV at 300 K
Sets the scale of the diode I–V curve. Increases ≈ 0.085 mV per K.
🔗
Contact potential
φ₁ − φ₂
eVcontact = φ1 − φ2
Potential appears at any junction between dissimilar metals — the thermocouple effect.
🧪
Standard electrode potential
E° · volt
E∘ = Ecathode − Eanode
Reference: standard hydrogen electrode E° = 0 V by definition.
⚗️
Nernst potential
ion equilibrium V
E= RTzF ln [ion+]out [ion+]in
Equilibrium potential when concentration gradient is balanced by electric field.
🧠
Resting membrane potential
≈ −70 mV
Vm = Vin − Vout ≈ −70 mV
Set by K⁺ leak channels — the neuron's "ready" voltage.
📈
Action potential
transient spike
Vpeak ≈ +40 mV , Vrest ≈ −70 mV
Depolarisation then repolarisation. All-or-nothing. Nerve signal speed.
🌀
Induced emf (Faraday's law)
ε = −dΦ/dt
ε=− dΦB dt
Minus sign = Lenz's law: induced emf opposes the change in flux.
🚄
Motional emf
ε = BLv
ε=BLv
The principle behind electric generators and railguns.
🌀
Planck voltage
≈ 1.04 × 10²⁷ V
VP = c4 4πε0G ≈ 1.04×1027 V
The voltage scale where quantum gravity becomes relevant.
