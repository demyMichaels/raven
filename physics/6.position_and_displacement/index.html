Electrochemistry · Intuition First

The Invisible Deposit

You pass a current through a metal-salt solution. Metal plates out onto the electrode — atom by atom, layer by layer.

But watch the electrode. Nothing seems to happen. No crust. No coating you can see.

Why is electroplating so slow? Because the number that governs it is almost unbelievably small. Let's go find it.

The Passage

Read it once. Don't solve it yet.

The electrochemical equivalent of a metal is 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ kg C⁻¹. The mass of the metal which 2.0 × 10⁴ C of electricity will deposit from a suitable electrolyte is…

Most students jump straight to multiplying. Don't. First, answer this:

How heavy is 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ kg? If you can't picture it, the answer you calculate won't mean anything.

Notice three things:

1. At 1 coulomb, the deposit is invisible — less than a millionth of a gram.
2. You need thousands of coulombs before the metal layer becomes visible to the eye.
3. At 20,000 C, you've only deposited about 2.6 grams. That's a paperclip's worth.

Now read the passage number out loud:

“One coulomb of charge deposits about half a grain of salt's worth of metal.”

That's the anchor. Once you feel how tiny that is, the rest of the problem becomes obvious — you're going to need a lot of charge to see anything.

Now Do the Maths

m = Z × Q

Mass deposited = (mass per coulomb) × (coulombs passed).

Z1.3 × 10⁻⁷ kg C⁻¹
Q2.0 × 10⁴ C
m = ZQ1.3 × 10⁻⁷ × 2.0 × 10⁴
1.3 × 2.0 = 2.6
10⁻⁷ × 10⁴ = 10⁻³
m2.6 × 10⁻³ kg

Convert to grams: 2.6 × 10⁻³ kg = 2.6 g.

Answer
Correct Answer
2.6 × 10⁻³ kg

= 2.6 grams ≈ one paperclip

Twenty thousand coulombs — a hefty charge — bought you a paperclip. That's electrochemistry's reality: the deposit rate is minuscule, so industrial plating runs for hours at high current.

Lock It In

What you actually learned

  • ✓Z is tiny. For metals it sits around 10⁻⁷ to 10⁻⁶ kg per coulomb — a grain of salt, not a gram.
  • ✓m = ZQ. Mass deposited is just the per-coulomb amount times the total charge.
  • ✓Charge, not current, deposits mass. Q = It, so slowing the current and running longer changes nothing.
  • ✓Anchors beat memorisation. "Half a grain of salt per coulomb" tells you instantly that the answer must be grams, never kilograms.

Next time you meet a small number, don't calculate first. Ask: what real object weighs this much? Then the arithmetic has somewhere to land.
