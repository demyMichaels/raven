Is the DO + local stack sustainable for 100+ countries?

DigitalOcean operates 16 datacenters across 13 regions — no presence in South America, the Middle East, or most of Africa. For 100+ countries, latency and data residency become binding constraints. The stack works to ~8 countries; beyond that, a hybrid DO + AWS architecture is required.

LATENCY

Latency & data residency as countries scale

When the stack breaks — and why.

⏱️ Latency to nearest DO region ms

Countries beyond 8 experience >150ms latency — unacceptable for live conversation and real-time role-play. AWS regions cut this to <80ms.

⚖️ Countries requiring data residency

At 15 countries, GDPR, PDPA, LGPD and similar laws force in-country or in-region data storage — DO cannot serve all of them.

DECISION

When to upgrade — decision framework

Three binding constraints, three upgrade actions.

TRIGGER 1

⏱️ Latency SLA breach

When >20% of users are more than 150ms from the nearest DO region.

  • Add AWS regions: Bahrain, São Paulo, Singapore, Mumbai, Cape Town
  • Deploy AWS Global Accelerator for anycast routing
  • Keep compute on DO where latency is acceptable

Triggers at: 8 countries

TRIGGER 2

⚖️ Data residency non-compliance

When a country mandates in-country data storage for children's data.

  • Add AWS local zones in regulated countries
  • Use Kasi Cloud (Nigeria), Nxtra (India), etc. for in-country
  • Keep DO for non-regulated markets

Triggers at: 15 countries

TRIGGER 3

💰 Egress cost exceeds AWS TCO

When CDN egress cost on DO exceeds the total AWS equivalent cost.

  • DO Spaces egress: $0.01/GB — AWS S3: $0.09/GB
  • But DO has no global CDN edge — origin pulls cost more
  • Migrate video to CloudFront at ~$0.085/GB US/EU, ~$0.12/GB other regions

Triggers at: 30 countries
